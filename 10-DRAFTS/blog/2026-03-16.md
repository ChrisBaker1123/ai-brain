## H2 Structure and Outline

### Introduction (~150 words)

Open with the stat: 63% of RIAs now use some form of AI (Schwab 2026 Study), up from roughly 30% two years ago. But only 1 in 10 have fully integrated AI into their workflows.

The gap is not awareness — it is implementation. Most advisors know AI can help. They just don't know where to start without creating compliance risk or adding another complex tool to their stack.

This guide covers the practical steps to go from "AI-curious" to "AI-productive" — using tools you probably already have.

### H2: Start with What You Already Have (~200 words)

Key points:
- Most advisory firms already have access to AI through Microsoft 365 Copilot, which is bundled with many enterprise licenses
- You don't need a new vendor, a new login, or a new compliance review to start using Copilot for writing tasks
- The barrier to AI adoption is not technology — it is knowing what to ask the AI to do
- Frame the problem: AI is a blank canvas. Without structure, advisors get generic output that doesn't match their voice or compliance needs
- Transition: this is where templates come in

### H2: The Three Workflows Where AI Saves the Most Time (~250 words)

Frame around the Copytalk/FPA study: advisors spend 41% of a 53-hour week on administrative tasks.

**1. Meeting Prep and Follow-Up**
- Before: 1-4 hours per client meeting pulling notes, building agendas, writing summaries
- After: Pre-built template structures the AI output — client brief, talking points, follow-up email
- The template handles format and compliance guardrails. You add the client-specific details.

**2. Client Communication**
- Routine emails (annual reviews, market updates, onboarding welcome) follow predictable structures
- Templates give the AI the right tone, length, and compliance references for each scenario
- Personalization comes from the advisor, not the AI — you fill in the details that matter

**3. Content and Prospecting**
- LinkedIn posts, referral request emails, COI outreach — these are the tasks advisors know they should do but never have time for
- A content creation template turns "write a LinkedIn post" into a structured request with audience, topic, and tone already defined

### H2: The Data Question — What Goes Where (~200 words)

Key points:
- The biggest compliance concern with AI is data handling, not the AI output itself
- Three tiers of AI data risk:
  1. **Tools that upload your data** (meeting recorders, CRM integrations) — require full vendor evaluation
  2. **Tools where you type client data in** (ChatGPT, Copilot) — governed by your firm's acceptable use policy
  3. **Tools where client data never enters** (copy-paste template libraries) — lowest risk surface
- Explain the copy-paste model: the template is the instruction set. You paste it into your firm-approved AI tool. Client details are added inside that tool's environment, governed by your existing policies.
- Reference: "compliance-aware, not compliance-approved" — templates include regulatory references but don't constitute legal advice

---

### H2: How to Evaluate an AI Tool for Your Practice (~200 words)

Five questions to ask before adopting any AI tool:

1. **Where does my client data go?** Does it leave my firm's environment? Is it used for model training?
2. **Does it work with my existing tools?** Integration with Microsoft 365, Redtail, Wealthbox matters more than a flashy standalone app
3. **What is the actual time savings?** Request a specific workflow walkthrough, not a demo of features
4. **What does my compliance officer need to review?** If the answer is "nothing" because no data is uploaded, that is a significant advantage
5. **What happens when I cancel?** Avoid 12-month lock-ins. Look for month-to-month pricing with no penalties

### H2: Getting Started This Week (~150 words)

Practical action steps:

1. **Today**: Pick one repetitive writing task you did this week (a follow-up email, meeting prep, CRM note)
2. **Tomorrow**: Open Microsoft Copilot and try writing that task with a plain-language request. Notice where the output is too generic.
3. **This week**: Try using a pre-built template instead of writing the request from scratch. Compare the output quality.

The difference between "AI didn't work for me" and "AI saves me 10 hours a week" is almost always the quality of the input. Templates solve the input problem.
