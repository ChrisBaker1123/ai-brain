# Sneaker Bot Era

#personal #business #origin

> Where the tech instinct was born. Running automation at scale before I could drive.

---

## What It Was

In high school, I ran sneaker bots — automated software that purchases limited-release sneakers the moment they drop, faster than any human can click. The sneakers I secured at retail ($150–$250) resold for $300–$2,000+ depending on the release. It was an arbitrage business powered by technology.

This wasn't casual. It was a real operation.

---

## The Bots I Ran

### Bath Bot
One of the bots in my rotation. Likely a private or invite-only bot distributed through Discord cook groups — many of the most effective bots never appeared on mainstream review sites and were only available through community connections. Getting access to these private bots was part of the competitive advantage.

### Trickle (Wrath)
Trickle and Wrath were two separate bots, both elite-tier:

**Trickle Bot**: A command-line-based bot, particularly strong on Yeezy Supply, Adidas, and Shopify sites. $499 initial + $50/month. Only available through random Twitter restocks, third-party marketplaces, and Discord group buys — never freely purchasable. One user famously claimed 1,132 pairs on Yeezy Day 2021. It featured proprietary anti-bot-protection bypassing that adapted in real time.

**Wrath Bot (Wrath AIO)**: One of the most elite and coveted sneaker bots ever made. All-in-one supporting US/Canadian Footsites, Shopify, Supreme, and Yeezy Supply. Only ~300 copies ever released. Resale peaked at ~$10,000 in September 2021, typically trading at $4,000–$7,000 on BotMart and SplashMarket. Proxyway called it "potentially the only bot you need."

### Mech Bot
Likely **MEK AIO** (by MEK Robotics, "Make Everyone Cook"). Launched 2019, supported Shopify, Footsites, Yeezy Supply, Nike SNKRS. $300 initial + $45/month, aftermarket resale ~$1,900. Considered versatile and beginner-friendly. MEK Robotics also made MEKpreme for Supreme drops.

### Others
The bot landscape shifted constantly. New bots launched, old ones died, updates broke things, sites added anti-bot measures. Staying competitive meant staying current — testing new tools, joining cook groups, sharing intelligence with other botters. The sneaker resale market peaked at $6–10B+ in 2020–2021 before cooling in 2022.

## The Operation

Running bots wasn't just "install and press go." It was a technology stack:

**Proxies**: You need residential proxies so the site doesn't detect that 50 checkout attempts are coming from the same IP. Proxy management was its own skill — rotating IPs, testing speed, managing pools.

**Servers**: Speed matters at the millisecond level. Running bots on a local machine wasn't fast enough. Cloud servers closer to the site's data center gave a speed advantage.

**Payment Profiles**: Each checkout needs a unique payment method and shipping address. Managing dozens of profiles, each with a different card and jigged address, was operational complexity.

**Timing**: Drops happen at specific times. Being ready — bots configured, proxies tested, profiles loaded — before the drop was critical. Miss the first 5 seconds and you miss the shoe.

**Cook Groups**: Private Discord servers where botters shared tips, monitor links, restock alerts, and early site information. Information asymmetry was an advantage.

## What I Learned

This era taught me five things that directly apply to everything I build today:

### 1. Automation Is Leverage
One person with the right bot can outperform a thousand people refreshing a page manually. This is the same insight behind Advisor Intelligence: one advisor with the right templates can produce better output faster than one without them. Technology multiplies human effort.

### 2. Speed Wins
In botting, milliseconds determined success. In business, speed to market matters. I built Advisor Intelligence and shipped it live before competitors could emerge. First-mover advantage is real when you move fast enough.

### 3. Supply/Demand Is Universal
Sneakers are artificially scarce. AI expertise for financial advisors is naturally scarce. The pattern is the same: limited supply + high demand = margin. Find the scarcity, provide the supply.

### 4. Systems Thinking
Botting required managing multiple interdependent systems: bots, proxies, servers, profiles, timing, intelligence. If one piece failed, the whole operation failed. I learned to think in systems — which is exactly how I think about product architecture, marketing funnels, and business strategy now.

### 5. Technology Is the Multiplier
The kids who bought sneakers manually lost. The kids who had better tools won. The same is true in every industry. Advisors who use AI well will outperform those who don't. I'm building the tools that make that happen.
