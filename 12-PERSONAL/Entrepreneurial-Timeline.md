# Entrepreneurial Timeline

#personal #business #timeline

> Every step, from Shopify stores to financial advising. The path only makes sense looking backward.

## The Timeline

### Early Teens (~2018–2019) — Shopify Stores
- Built and ran e-commerce stores on Shopify
- No mentor, no trigger — just innate curiosity about online business
- Learned: product sourcing, digital marketing, online payments, customer service
- **Lesson**: You can build a business from your bedroom if you understand the internet

### High School (~2019–2022) — Sneaker Bots & Reselling
- Ran automated purchasing bots: **Bath**, **Trickle Wrath**, **Mech**, and others
- Purchased limited-release sneakers at retail using automation, resold at markup
- Required: proxy management, server optimization, payment profile management, timing
- Real money, real operations, real technology stack
- **Lesson**: Automation is leverage. Speed wins. Supply/demand arbitrage is everywhere.
- See [[Sneaker-Bot-Era]]

### High School — Meeting Mark Linder
- Showed Mark Linder the bot operation
- Linder was impressed by the systems thinking, not the sneakers
- This connection led directly to Fowler Scholars admission at SDSU
- **Lesson**: The right person seeing the right thing at the right time changes trajectories
- See [[Mentor-Mark-Linder]]

### 2024 — SDSU & Fowler Scholars
- Entered SDSU Fowler College of Business, Class of 2028, finance major
- Admitted to Fowler Scholars through Mark Linder's connection
- Gained access to mentors (Dan Skiles, others), internship opportunities, and a network of business leaders
- See [[Mentor-Dan-Skiles]]

### 2025 — Sprouts Internship
- Internship at Sprouts Farmers Market, facilitated through Mark Linder / Fowler Scholars
- Focus: capturing Gen Z market share
- Applied understanding of how young consumers discover, evaluate, and purchase
- **Lesson**: I'm positioned as a Gen Z market expert — companies pay for this perspective

### 2025–2026 — Taylor Farms Internship
- Current internship at Taylor Farms (Taylor Fresh Foods)
- Focus: Gen Z market share strategy (same positioning as Sprouts)
- Continued building the "young person who understands both generations" brand
- **Lesson**: The Gen Z expert positioning has legs across industries, not just tech

### 2025–Present — Advisor Intelligence (Phase 1)
- Built a copy-paste AI template toolkit for financial advisors
- Core insight: dad is a financial advisor → advisors need help with AI → I know both worlds
- 68 templates, 8 workflow categories, AI Coach chatbot, documents, tutorials, action plans
- Live at https://www.advisorintelligence.app
- First real client: Will Jones (Wells Fargo, SDSU alum)
- Priority prospect: DLK Investment Management (~$350M AUM)
- **Lesson**: Stick to what you know. Your niche advantage is your moat.
- See [[Overview]], [[Client-Pipeline]], [[DLK-Engagement]]

### Future — AI Architecture Installations (Phase 2)
- Next evolution: deploying AI systems (Claude bots, OpenClaw) directly into businesses
- Target: the advisors and firms who already trust me from Phase 1
- Sell the infrastructure, not just the templates
- **Lesson (anticipated)**: Trust earned in Phase 1 converts to bigger contracts in Phase 2

### Future — Financial Advisor (Phase 3)
- End goal: become a financial advisor, managing wealth for the people I've been serving
- Target age: 25 (exit Advisor Intelligence, transition to advising)
- "I already built and installed your AI systems, you trust me, now let me manage your wealth"
- **Lesson (anticipated)**: The longest game wins. Every phase builds on the last.
- See [[Business-Strategy-Master-Plan]]

## The Pattern

Every stage shares the same DNA:
1. **See a gap** — something people need that nobody's providing well
2. **Build a system** — use technology to fill the gap faster/better than manual work
3. **Compound the advantage** — each success opens the door to the next opportunity

Shopify → bots → Fowler Scholars → internships → Advisor Intelligence → AI architecture → financial advising. One thread, pulled forward.
