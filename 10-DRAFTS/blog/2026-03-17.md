## H1: Why Financial Advisors Need an AI Consultant — Not Another AI Tool

### Introduction (~150 words)
- Open with the stat: 63% of RIAs now use AI, but only 1 in 10 have fully integrated it (Schwab 2026)
- The access problem is solved — every advisor has Copilot or ChatGPT available
- The real problem is implementation: knowing where AI fits in a specific practice, what workflows benefit most, and how to use it without compliance risk
- Thesis: advisory firms don't need another tool. They need a person who understands both AI and how advisors work.

### H2: The IT Analogy — We've Seen This Before (~200 words)
- When businesses first got computers in the '90s, they didn't buy software and figure it out alone
- They hired an IT person (or outsourced IT) — someone who came in periodically, set things up, trained the staff, maintained the systems
- AI is at the same inflection point for advisory firms
- The firms that figure out implementation first gain a compounding advantage in efficiency and client experience
- Unlike software that installs itself, AI requires workflow design, prompt engineering, and ongoing optimization — human work

### H2: Why "Just Use ChatGPT" Doesn't Work for Advisors (~200 words)
- Generic AI tools produce generic output — advisors need communication that matches their voice, practice style, and compliance environment
- Compliance isn't a checkbox — it's a workflow consideration at every step (what data you enter, what language you use, how you document)
- Advisors spend 41% of their 53-hour work week on admin tasks (Copytalk/FPA), but knowing which tasks to automate requires practice-specific knowledge
- The "I tried it once and the output was useless" problem: without structured templates and workflow context, AI underdelivers
- Microsoft Copilot is the primary AI tool in most firms — advisors need guidance specific to their existing tools, not another platform

### H2: What an AI Consulting Engagement Actually Looks Like (~250 words)
- **Month 1 — Audit & Setup**: Review the firm's current tech stack, identify the 3-5 workflows where AI has the highest ROI, set up templates and processes
- **Ongoing — Build & Train**: Create custom AI workflows for the team, train staff on execution, document processes so the firm owns its systems
- **Continuous — Optimize & Update**: AI tools change fast. A consulting relationship means the firm stays current without dedicating internal resources to tracking every update
- Contrast with SaaS: a tool gives you features. A consultant gives you implementation, training, and ongoing strategy.
- The toolkit as a resource: 68 copy-paste templates across 8 workflow categories (meeting prep, follow-ups, client emails, prospecting, content creation, CRM notes, onboarding, practice operations) — included in every engagement, not sold separately

### H2: The ROI Case for AI Consulting (~200 words)
- Hartford Funds data: advisors save 10+ hours/week with AI tools when properly implemented
- At $200/hr effective advisor time, that's $8,000+/month in recovered capacity
- Compare to consulting investment: even at the highest tier, the ROI math works if it saves the team just a few hours per week
- Recovered time goes to client-facing work — the activities that actually grow AUM
- Secondary ROI: better client communication quality, faster follow-ups, more consistent onboarding experience
- The cost of doing nothing: 46% of advisors are still "experimenting" with AI (FSI Survey 2026). The firms that move from experimenting to implementing gain a structural advantage.

### H2: Is This Right for Your Firm? (~150 words)
- Ideal fit: independent RIAs managing $150M–$750M in AUM
- You have AI tools available but haven't integrated them into daily workflows
- Your team has tried AI but found the output generic or unhelpful
- You want to use AI for communication and admin — not replace your planning, tax, or portfolio software
- You value a relationship over a subscription — someone who understands your practice, not a dashboard
- Not a fit: firms looking for portfolio analysis, financial planning, or risk assessment software (we complement those tools, not compete with them)
